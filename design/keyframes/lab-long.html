One search, two evaluators.

Both sides of every match use the same alpha-beta search, which I wrote from scratch in TypeScript. Only the judgement at the end of each line changes: handcrafted tables, or a small neural net I trained.

Handcrafted

The PeSTO piece-square tables by Ronald Friederich: a score for every piece on every square. Below, the knight in the middlegame. Not trained by me; credited.

Learned (NNUE)

768 inputs (every piece on every square), two 256-wide accumulators, one for each side, then 32, then one number. Updated incrementally as moves are made, so the search can afford it.

Twenty million positions.

The labels are public Stockfish evaluations from the Lichess evaluation database (CC0). No Stockfish network weights are copied. The filters keep only quiet positions that a leaf evaluation should be able to judge.

Kept: dedupe by position, ply 10 or later, no en passant, not in check, first engine move not a capture, no mates, clamped to ±1500 cp, depth 12 or more.

Trained twice.

PyTorch, mean squared error in win/draw/loss space, quantisation-aware from the second epoch, exported to int8 and run in WebAssembly inside a worker: 410 KB. The first net learned too little. The retrain used more, deeper data.

A second check guards against mimicry: a net that simply copies PeSTO can never beat it. The final net correlates 0.70 with PeSTO, well short of 1.0.

First, check the referee.

Before any result counts, the harness plays handcrafted against itself: 100 games at 50,000 nodes, every opening played from both sides. 17 wins, 66 draws, 17 losses: exactly 0.0 Elo. The harness is fair, so any later gap belongs to the evaluator.

Then the match.

Learned against handcrafted, same search, 50,000 nodes a move, fifty openings from both sides. The test was set up to stop as soon as the evidence was decisive (SPRT, H0 = 0 Elo, H1 = +10 Elo). It stopped at game 128.

What failed.

What was tested as "Learned" is not the net alone. It is the material count plus the net's output, clamped to ±60 centipawns: the net can move a position by at most 0.6 of a pawn either way.

Two causes, neither isolated

The quality of the training data, or the integration itself: with a ±60 cp clamp on top of material, the net has little room to change a decision.

What I learned

A loss at the spec cap is still a result.

Do not rematch this net. A new net still has to pass the data and progress gates first.
